# Tri-temporal recipient delivery semantics, version 1

This is a prose specification for a separate reference implementation. It does not prescribe an index, ranking model or programming technique. The reference author's inputs are this document and data adhering to it; the author must not inspect application code or existing fixtures. Return candidate item identities and state from metadata alone. Retrieval relevance is a separate question: an available irrelevant item is not gold support simply because it is available.

## Records and identity

A ledger contains assertions, evidence passages, lifecycle events and receipt records. Every item has a unique identity within its item type and a ledger recording timestamp. An assertion contains subject, predicate, object, summary, world, plane, perspective, valid_from, optional valid_to, recorded_at, and evidence passages. An evidence passage has its own id, text, title, recorded_at and parent assertion identity. Its delivery never implies delivery of the parent's claim, and parent delivery never implies delivery of its passages. Evidence identity in the stored fixture may be explicit; if absent it is the parent's identity followed by `-e` and a one-based passage position. A lifecycle event has id, assertion_id, type, effective_at, recorded_at, reason, source and, for correct/supersede, replacement_id. Replacement identity does not grant access to the replacement.

A receipt contains id, recipient_id, item_type, item_id, received_at and recorded_at. It asserts receipt of that exact item by that recipient at received_at, with the receipt log itself becoming known at recorded_at. received_at must not exceed the receipt's recorded_at. An item can have multiple receipts; any qualifying receipt suffices. Item existence and receipt existence are separately constrained by the query's knowledge boundary. Receipt logs may arrive later than the delivery they describe. The source item's recording time can be later than its receipt: no query can use that source item before the source enters this ledger. Unknown item references and duplicate item identities are invalid input. Recipients are identities distinct from sources, subjects and assertion perspectives.

## Coordinates and boundaries

A query identifies recipient_id and three independent coordinates: valid_at (the time in the world being asked about), known_at (the ledger state available), and received_by (latest delivery time available to the recipient). The coordinates need not be equal or ordered. Dates and timestamps are normalized to UTC with microsecond precision; timezone offsets representing the same instant are equal. A date without a time means midnight UTC. Input without a timezone is treated as UTC.

Recording and delivery cutoffs are inclusive: source recorded_at and receipt recorded_at must both be at or before known_at, and receipt received_at must be at or before received_by. An item's assertion validity interval is half-open: valid_from is included and valid_to is excluded. An absent valid_to means no upper boundary. Source recording and receipt visibility are checked before lifecycle projection. A future or unreceived event cannot affect the recipient's projection at any valid_at.

World, plane and perspective query filters match exactly unless the requested filter is `all`. The fixture queries use explicit filter strings. Retrieval limit, scores and query wording do not change candidate visibility. Setting include_retired permits otherwise scoped retired assertions; it never bypasses receipts, ledger cutoff, or the assertion's original validity interval.

## Assertions and lifecycle notices

Begin with assertions whose original validity intervals contain valid_at, whose source recording times pass known_at, whose metadata match the query filters, and whose own receipts pass the two receipt cutoffs. For each, consider only events addressed to that assertion that pass both source recording and event receipt visibility. Events are applied in ascending effective_at order; ties use supersede before correct before retract, then recorded_at, then lexical event id. Each event effective at or before valid_at sets status to superseded, corrected or retracted respectively. Without such an event status is active. Supersede also bounds effective_valid_to by its effective_at, even when that boundary is later than valid_at; the bound is the minimum of all visible supersede times and original valid_to. Correct and retract change status once effective, without rewriting the original interval.

With include_retired false, only active assertions before effective_valid_to remain candidates. A correction or retraction can remove the old assertion even if its replacement has not arrived. The result can therefore contain no active claim. A replacement is independently eligible only from its own validity, recording, scope and receipts. If both claims arrive but their connecting lifecycle event has not arrived, retain both active claims as unresolved alternatives. Do not silently select the globally newest one. Receipt of a notice does not imply receipt of the old assertion, its passages, or the replacement.

## Evidence and event item views

For an assertion context, attach only its separately received evidence passages with source recorded_at at or before known_at. Hidden passage text, titles and metadata must not enter embeddings, lexical scoring, graph expansion or caches for that context. Attach only visible events for that assertion, including known notices effective in the future; future notices do not retire it prematurely.

A separate item view supports standalone evidence and lifecycle notices. An evidence passage can be a candidate when its parent claim was never delivered or is retired. Apply the parent assertion's world/plane/perspective and original validity interval as explicit evidence scope, but do not output or imply the parent's undelivered claim text or lifecycle status. The returned item is the passage and its own identity, with no inferred endorsement. This is a deliberate evidence scope convention to be stated in results, not a universal rule for document truth.

A lifecycle notice can be returned in an explicit notice query even when its parent claim is no longer active or was not delivered. The notice must pass source and receipt cutoffs and the target assertion's world/plane/perspective filters. Its effective_at may be before or after valid_at; the notice concerns that change and must state its effective date. Do not require the parent assertion's validity interval to contain valid_at for this notice view. Source and reason text belonging to the received notice are available; undelivered replacement text is not. Event-only gold is scored as notice retrieval, not as no-support abstention.

## Gold and independence

Author gold from a scenario's stated question and the above semantics. Gold assertion IDs refer to relevant eligible claims, required passage IDs to their relevant support, standalone passage IDs to evidence-only support, and event IDs to relevant notices for notice questions. An empty gold set means the question cannot be answered from the required view, not that the recipient has no unrelated available items. Preserve multiple conflicting supported alternatives when the question requests the recipient's available reports. Mark question kind and expected answer explicitly.

Before scoring, record the specification hash, reference author identity and exact brief. Record every discrepancy between candidate projections, including empty discrepancy logs. Classify a mismatch as implementation defect, reference defect or specification ambiguity with a concrete case and resolution. No implementation is privileged merely because it existed first. Specification changes require a versioned amendment and new reference output.
